# Illustration prompts for Gemini

Black-and-white pencil cartoons of the teacher and the pupils, one for each
place listed in `docs/illustrations.md`. Each picture shows what happens in
the lesson, plus **one gag**: a child, the teacher or the cat doing
something mischievous at the same moment.

## How to use these

1. **Make the character sheet first** (prompt 0). Save the result. Attach it
   to every later prompt and add the line *"Keep the characters consistent
   with the attached character sheet."* Gemini keeps faces far more
   consistent with a reference image than from words alone.
2. **Paste the style block** at the start of every prompt.
3. **Never ask for writing in the picture.** Image models garble Greek
   letters. Every prompt asks for blank tablets and a blank board; if a page
   needs Greek on the board, add it in the layout afterwards.
4. **Aspect ratio** is given for each prompt to fit its space on the page.
   The page is 5.3 inches wide, so 3:2 prints about 3.5 inches high, 2:1
   about 2.6 inches, and 4:3 about 4 inches.
5. **If the gag takes over**, add *"The main action is in the centre; the
   joke is small and to one side."* The reader should see the scene first
   and find the joke second.

## Rules for the jokes

- **One gag per picture.** Two gags make a cartoon busy.
- **The joke comes from the story.** Every gag below is something these
  characters would do, and most are taken from the lesson's own text: Felix
  writing a letter to the cat, the cat as lord of the courtyard, the tablet
  that falls in Lesson 14.
- **Nobody is laughed at for being wrong.** The book treats every mistake as
  a reasonable one, so the jokes are about mischief, never about a child
  getting Greek wrong.
- **The Scripture lessons keep their jokes away from the text.** Theodoros
  does not let the class make fun of sacred passages, so in Part IV the
  humour is in the children and the cat, not in the book they are reading.
- **Optional running gag: find the cat.** The cat appears in about half the
  prompts. If you like, add *"and somewhere small in the picture, the cat"*
  to the rest, so that pupils can hunt for it on every page.

## Style block

> Black-and-white pencil cartoon: a lively graphite line drawing with
> expressive, slightly exaggerated faces and poses, clean outlines, light
> hatching for shadow, no colour, plain white background, generous white
> space around the figures. Warm and witty, in the tradition of
> school-story cartoons; the humour is gentle and never cruel. A simple
> composition with few figures and little background. Setting: a small
> schoolroom in a private house in Rome in AD 400, opening onto a courtyard
> with plain columns. Late-Roman details: knee-length long-sleeved tunics
> with two narrow vertical stripes, sandals, low wooden stools, wax tablets
> made of two hinged wooden leaves held on the knees, slim styluses, a
> whitened wooden board on an easel, scrolls on a wall shelf. No desks, no
> blackboard, no paper books, no togas, nothing medieval or monastic. No
> text, letters or writing anywhere: tablets and board are blank or show
> only faint indistinct marks.

## The people (and the cat)

The book gives ages but almost no appearance. These descriptions are
invented only to keep the drawings consistent; change them freely, but then
change them everywhere.

## 0 · Character sheet (make this first)

> [Style block] A character reference sheet: ten figures in a row on a
> white background, evenly spaced, each facing slightly toward the viewer.
> From left: a Greek teacher about 45, lean, short dark beard going grey,
> receding hair, long tunic with a plain cloak over one shoulder, arms
> folded; a boy of 13 with short curly hair and sharp features, leaning
> back; a girl of 14 with braided pinned-up hair, holding her tablet
> neatly; a small boy of 12 with untidy hair, balancing his wax tablet on
> his head; a girl of 12 with long hair tied back, mid-gesture as if
> telling a story; a tall thin boy of 14, gazing upward thoughtfully; a
> girl of 14 with her hair in a simple knot, watching the others; a
> round-faced boy of 12 with cropped hair, standing very straight; a girl
> of 13 with dark curly hair tied with a ribbon, grinning; and at the end a
> scruffy tabby cat with a notched ear, sitting, unimpressed by all of them.
> Aspect ratio 3:1, wide.

## Front matter

**1 · To the Student (end, p. 6)** — 4:3
> [Style block] A girl of 14 with her hair in a simple knot sits on a stool,
> staring at the wax tablet on her knees with sudden delight: she has just
> read it. Behind her a small untidy-haired boy has fallen asleep with his
> cheek stuck to his own tablet. The bearded teacher, far in the
> background, notices the girl and ignores the boy. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**2 · The School on the Caelian Hill (end, p. 8)** — 4:3
> [Style block] The whole schoolroom seen from the courtyard doorway: the
> bearded teacher beside a whitened board on an easel, eight children aged
> 12 to 14 on low stools in a loose half-circle with wax tablets on their
> knees, scrolls on a wall shelf. The gag: a small untidy-haired boy is
> dangling a stylus on a thread behind his stool, and a tabby cat is
> crouched to pounce on it. The teacher is very deliberately not looking.
> Aspect ratio 4:3.

**3 · How This Course Pronounces Greek (end, p. 13)** — 4:3
> [Style block] The bearded teacher holds a small lit oil lamp in front of
> his mouth and breathes a sound at it; the flame leans politely away.
> Beside him a small untidy-haired boy tries the same sound on his own lamp
> with puffed-out cheeks, and blows it out completely, with a curl of
> smoke. The other pupils lean back in mock alarm. Aspect ratio 4:3.

## Part title pages

All 4:3, about 3.6 inches high.

**4 · Part I · The Strange Language (p. 16)**
> [Style block] The bearded teacher, seen from behind and to one side,
> writes large faint capital letters on the whitened board. Seven pupils
> watch the board. The gag: a curly-haired boy has tipped his stool back on
> two legs and is staring at the ceiling, balanced at the exact point of
> falling over. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**5 · Part II · The Machinery of Greek (p. 67)**
> [Style block] Pupils on stools bend over the wax tablets on their knees,
> copying. The bearded teacher walks slowly between the stools with his
> hands behind his back. The gag: just as he passes, a small untidy-haired
> boy hurriedly covers with his elbow a doodle on his tablet: a bearded
> face with a long nose that looks exactly like the teacher. Aspect ratio
> 4:3.

**6 · Part III · The World of the Classics (p. 119)**
> [Style block] The bearded teacher sits reading aloud from an open scroll.
> The pupils sit around him listening; a girl with long hair tied back
> leans forward, completely absorbed. The gag: behind the teacher's back, a
> small untidy-haired boy covers one eye with his hand and looms over a
> round-faced boy, playing the one-eyed giant, while the round-faced boy
> pretends not to be scared. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**7 · Part IV · Greek Becomes a Language of Scripture (p. 179)**
> [Style block] The bearded teacher holds open a thick bound book with
> wooden covers (a late-Roman codex) to show the class. A tall thin boy
> stands in front, seeing it perfectly. The gag: a short round-faced boy
> behind him cannot see past him, so he has climbed onto a stool and is
> peering over the tall boy's shoulder on tiptoe, wobbling. Aspect ratio
> 4:3.

**8 · Part V · Becoming a Reader (p. 239)**
> [Style block] In the courtyard, a boy stands reading aloud from an
> unrolled scroll. The other pupils sit on the steps listening, and the
> bearded teacher leans against a column, arms folded. The gag: on the wall
> directly above the reader sits the tabby cat, looking down at him with
> the stern expression of a strict examiner. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**9 · Part VI · Writing What You Read (p. 298)**
> [Style block] Close view: the bearded teacher leans over a seated girl's
> shoulder and points at her wax tablet. The gag: the thing he is pointing
> at is a neat cat's paw print pressed into the wax, and the cat is walking
> away out of the picture with its tail in the air. The girl looks
> outraged; the teacher looks interested. Aspect ratio 4:3.

## Lesson ends

**10 · Lesson 1, The Unwelcome Tablet (p. 23)** — 4:3 · student edition only
> [Style block] The bearded teacher holds up a boy's wax tablet and looks at
> it in a long, terrible silence. The curly-haired boy who wrote it sits
> with an innocent face. The gag: the whole class has frozen, and a small
> untidy-haired boy is watching through his fingers as if at a gladiator
> show. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**11 · Lesson 2, The Case of the Missing Letters (p. 30)** — 3:2
> [Style block] End of the lesson. A girl of 14 with her hair in a knot asks
> the bearded teacher a quiet, pointed question, and he looks caught out.
> The gag: in the background, unnoticed by either of them, the tabby cat is
> sitting squarely on another pupil's open wax tablet, smudging it. Aspect
> ratio 3:2.

**12 · Lesson 3, Names and Nouns (p. 38)** — 3:2
> [Style block] Pupils tie small labels onto things around the room: a
> stool, a tablet, the window. A small untidy-haired boy pins a label onto a
> tall thin boy, who stands like a statue, bearing it with dignity. The
> gag: the cat has a label tied to its tail and is spinning round trying to
> catch it. The bearded teacher watches, trying not to smile. Aspect ratio
> 3:2.

**13 · Lesson 4, Who Did What? (p. 47)** — 3:2
> [Style block] The classroom set up as a law court. A girl of 12 with long
> hair sits as judge on two stools stacked on top of each other, holding a
> broom like a staff of office. A small boy stands as the witness with one
> hand on his heart, overacting wildly. A curly-haired boy, the accused, is
> yawning. The bearded teacher leans on the wall with his arms folded.
> Aspect ratio 3:2.

**14 · Lesson 5, The Verb Appears (p. 56)** — 2:1
> [Style block] The pupils take turns standing to introduce themselves.
> The gag: a small untidy-haired boy, whose turn it is, is standing on top
> of his stool to look taller, chest out. The bearded teacher, seated,
> points him silently back down to the floor. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**15 · Lesson 6, The First Real Sentence (p. 66)** — 4:3
> [Style block] End of the lesson. The bearded teacher wipes the whitened
> board clean with a cloth, leaving one small patch in the corner. The gag:
> the cat sits on top of the easel, one paw placed firmly over that last
> corner, guarding it, glaring at the cloth. Pupils are leaving behind.
> Aspect ratio 4:3.

**16 · Lesson 7, The Article's Many Disguises (p. 75)** — 2:1
> [Style block] A curly-haired boy argues with the bearded teacher, flinging
> his arm out in a grand gesture; his own wax tablet is flying off his knee
> as he does it. Beside him his older sister, braided hair pinned up,
> calmly makes another tally mark on her tablet without looking up. Aspect
> ratio 2:1.

**17 · Lesson 9, The Dative and the Recipient (p. 92)** — 3:2
> [Style block] A small untidy-haired boy presents a folded letter to the
> bearded teacher with enormous ceremony, bowing so low that his nose nearly
> touches the floor. The teacher accepts it with equally exaggerated
> gravity, one eyebrow raised. Classmates grin behind their tablets. Aspect
> ratio 3:2.

**18 · Lesson 11, One Verb, Several Clues (p. 109)** — 4:3
> [Style block] Years later: a grown man of about 30 with short curly hair,
> now a teacher himself, stands at a window in a plain provincial
> schoolroom, his back half-turned to a small class, quietly moved. The
> gag, gentle: in the front row one small boy with untidy hair is balancing
> a stylus on his nose. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**19 · Lesson 12, The First Reading Challenge (p. 118)** — 2:1 · student edition only
> [Style block] Two small teams of pupils huddle over their wax tablets,
> whispering. The bearded teacher stands between them as judge. The gag: a
> small untidy-haired boy is craning across to peek at the other team's
> tablet, and a girl with her hair in a knot is hauling him back by the
> collar without even looking up. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**20 · Lesson 13, The Past Comes Alive (p. 127)** — 3:2 student only, or 3:1 for both
> [Style block] After the lesson, a tall thin boy explains a big idea to the
> bearded teacher with a grand philosopher's gesture. The teacher listens,
> amused. The gag: the boy's cloak has slipped off his shoulder onto the
> floor, and the cat has already curled up asleep in it. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**21 · Lesson 14, The Aorist Arrives (p. 136)** — 3:2
> [Style block] A curly-haired boy writes carefully on the back of his wax
> tablet while the bearded teacher glances down at it. The gag: behind them
> the cat has just leapt from the shelf of scrolls, and a wax tablet it
> knocked off is caught in mid-air, about to hit the floor. Neither of them
> has noticed yet. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**22 · Lesson 15, The Words of Homer (p. 146)** — 4:3
> [Style block] By the shelf of scrolls, a girl of 12 with long hair tied
> back begs the bearded teacher for something, wearing an upturned bowl as
> a warrior's helmet and holding a stylus like a spear. He holds a battered
> old scroll protectively against his chest, as if it were a relic.
> Aspect ratio 4:3.

**23 · Lesson 16, Odysseus and the Problem of Cleverness (p. 157)** — 2:1 student only, or 3:1 for both
> [Style block] A small untidy-haired boy has his hand up to ask a question;
> he has drawn a single large eye on his forehead in charcoal. The bearded
> teacher looks at him with weary patience. The class is laughing. Aspect
> ratio 2:1.

**24 · Lesson 18, The Trial of a Translation (p. 178)** — 4:3
> [Style block] The classroom as a trial, the bearded teacher seated as
> judge. At the back a girl of 14 with braided pinned-up hair has stood up
> to speak, nervous but steady, and every head has turned to her. The gag:
> in the middle of the room a curly-haired boy is frozen in the grand pose
> of an orator, arm still raised, abandoned by his audience. Aspect ratio
> 4:3.

**25 · Lesson 19, The Familiar Words Return (p. 188)** — 4:3
> [Style block] Through the open doorway, the tabby cat sits on the
> courtyard wall like an emperor on a throne. A small untidy-haired boy
> bows deeply to it. The bearded teacher stands beside him, gesturing at
> the cat as if introducing royalty. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**26 · Lesson 20, The Greek of the Septuagint (p. 197)** — 3:2
> [Style block] A girl of 13 with dark curly hair counts something on her
> fingers while talking to the bearded teacher, who strokes his beard. The
> gag: she has run out of fingers and has grabbed the hand of a small boy
> beside her to keep counting on his. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**27 · Lesson 21, The Sermon on the Mount (p. 206)** — 3:2
> [Style block] A small untidy-haired boy, very pleased with himself, has
> counted to eight on his fingers and, to be sure, has taken off his
> sandals and is checking on his toes. The bearded teacher stands over him
> with a patient, challenging look. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**28 · Lesson 23, Attic and Koine Face to Face (p. 228)** — 2:1
> [Style block] End of the lesson. The bearded teacher sits at ease on the
> end of a bench, sandals off, eating figs from a bowl, talking to a small
> untidy-haired boy, who stares at this unexpected sight of the teacher
> off duty. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**29 · Lesson 24, Two Worlds, One Language Family (p. 238)** — 3:2
> [Style block] In the courtyard, a tall thin boy and the bearded teacher
> talk seriously over a scroll held partly open between them. The gag: the
> loose end of the scroll dangles down, and the cat below is batting at it
> with one paw. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**30 · Lesson 25, The Grammar of Questions (p. 249)** — 3:2 · student edition only
> [Style block] A girl of 14 with her hair in a knot reads aloud from her
> tablet. The bearded teacher, smiling, gestures toward her as if thanking
> her in front of the class. The gag: a curly-haired boy in the front row
> looks utterly scandalised at someone being thanked. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**31 · Lesson 27, The Translator's Dilemma (p. 267)** — 3:1
> [Style block] A girl with braided pinned-up hair stands before the bearded
> teacher, a little defensive. Beside her a girl with her hair in a knot
> unrolls a scroll. The gag: the scroll has unrolled much too far, right
> across the floor and out through the door, and the cat is chasing the
> end of it. Aspect ratio 3:1.

**32 · Lesson 28, A Greek Letter from Rome (p. 276)** — 2:1
> [Style block] A girl of 13 with dark curly hair writes on a sheet of
> papyrus with a reed pen while the bearded teacher reads over her shoulder,
> nodding. The gag: behind them a small untidy-haired boy has written his
> own letter and is solemnly handing it to the cat, which is reading it.
> Aspect ratio 2:1.

**33 · Lesson 30, The Final Examination (p. 297)** — 3:2
> [Style block] Evening, long shadows. The bearded teacher goes to put a
> last scroll on the shelf. A small untidy-haired boy waits at the door to
> go home. The gag: the slot on the shelf where the scroll belongs is
> already occupied by the cat, curled up asleep in it. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**34 · Lesson 31, The Voice That Looks Back (p. 309)** — 3:1
> [Style block] A small untidy-haired boy, soaking wet, dripping into a
> puddle, writes on his wax tablet while the bearded teacher reads over his
> shoulder. Through the door, on the courtyard wall, the cat sits perfectly
> dry, washing itself with a smug expression. Aspect ratio 3:1.

**35 · Lesson 32, What Will Be (p. 322)** — 4:3
> [Style block] An empty classroom after the lesson. The bearded teacher
> takes a wax tablet down from the shelf and studies it. A curly-haired boy
> stands waiting with an innocent face, and fingers crossed behind his
> back. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**36 · Lesson 35, What Might Be (p. 354)** — 3:2
> [Style block] At the courtyard door a tall thin boy turns back to ask a
> question. The bearded teacher answers him, carrying a wobbling stack of
> wax tablets taller than his head, with the cat sitting calmly on the top.
> Aspect ratio 3:2.

**37 · Lesson 36, The Old Verbs (p. 365)** — 3:1
> [Style block] Ten years later: a young woman of about 24 with braided
> pinned-up hair, now a teacher herself, stands in her own small schoolroom
> reading a very short letter, quietly moved. The gag, gentle: behind her
> one boy with short curly hair has tipped his stool back on two legs and
> is staring at the ceiling, exactly as her brother once did. Aspect ratio
> 3:1.
