# Illustration prompts for Gemini

Black-and-white pencil drawings showing the teacher and the pupils, one simple
scene for each place listed in `docs/illustrations.md`. Pictures of objects,
maps and diagrams have been left out.

## How to use these

1. **Make the character sheet first** (prompt 0). Save the result. Attach it
   to every later prompt and add the line *"Keep the characters consistent
   with the attached character sheet."* Gemini keeps faces far more
   consistent with a reference image than from words alone.
2. **Paste the style block** at the start of every prompt. It is written out
   in full once, below, so that each prompt stays short.
3. **Never ask for writing in the picture.** Image models garble Greek
   letters. Every prompt asks for blank tablets and a blank board; if a page
   needs Greek on the board, add it in the layout afterwards.
4. **Aspect ratio** is given for each prompt to fit its space on the page.
   The page is 5.3 inches wide, so 3:2 prints about 3.5 inches high, 2:1
   about 2.6 inches, and 4:3 about 4 inches.
5. **Draw what happens, not who people are.** The book keeps the pupils'
   personalities for the class to discover, so the prompts describe actions
   and poses, not traits like "the clever one".

## Style block

> Black-and-white pencil drawing, graphite on white paper, loose confident
> lines with light hatching for shadow, no colour, plain white background,
> generous white space around the figures. A simple composition with few
> figures and little background detail, like an illustration in a good
> school textbook: not a cartoon, not photorealistic. Setting: a small
> schoolroom in a private house in Rome in AD 400, opening onto a courtyard
> with plain columns. Late-Roman details: knee-length long-sleeved tunics
> with two narrow vertical stripes, sandals, low wooden stools, wax tablets
> made of two hinged wooden leaves held on the knees, slim styluses, a
> whitened wooden board on an easel, scrolls on a wall shelf. No desks, no
> blackboard, no paper books, no togas, nothing medieval or monastic. No
> text, letters or writing anywhere: tablets and board are blank or show
> only faint indistinct marks.

## 0 · Character sheet (make this first)

> [Style block] A character reference sheet: nine full-length figures
> standing in a row on a white background, evenly spaced, each facing
> slightly toward the viewer. From left: a Greek teacher about 45, lean,
> short dark beard going grey, receding hair, long tunic with a plain cloak
> over one shoulder; a boy of 13 with short curly hair and sharp features; a
> girl of 14 with braided pinned-up hair; a small boy of 12 with untidy
> hair; a girl of 12 with long hair tied back; a tall thin boy of 14; a girl
> of 14 with her hair in a simple knot; a round-faced boy of 12 with cropped
> hair; a girl of 13 with dark curly hair tied with a ribbon. The children
> hold wax tablets. Aspect ratio 3:1, wide.

## Front matter

**1 · To the Student (end, p. 6)** — 4:3
> [Style block] A girl of 14 with her hair in a simple knot sits on a stool
> with a wax tablet on her knees, looking at it with sudden quiet surprise,
> as if she has just understood it. Other pupils are blurred and lightly
> sketched behind her. The teacher, bearded, stands far in the background,
> noticing. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**2 · The School on the Caelian Hill (end, p. 8)** — 4:3
> [Style block] The whole schoolroom seen from the courtyard doorway: the
> bearded teacher standing beside a whitened board on an easel, eight
> children aged 12 to 14 on low stools in a loose half-circle with wax
> tablets on their knees, a shelf of scrolls on the wall, and the courtyard
> columns in the foreground. Calm, ordinary morning. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**3 · How This Course Pronounces Greek (end, p. 13)** — 4:3
> [Style block] The bearded teacher holds a small lit oil lamp a hand's
> breadth from his mouth, saying a sound, and the flame is bending away
> from his breath. Three pupils lean in to watch the flame closely; one of
> them is trying it with her own hand in front of her mouth. Aspect ratio
> 4:3.

## Part title pages

All 4:3, about 3.6 inches high.

**4 · Part I · The Strange Language (p. 16)**
> [Style block] The bearded teacher, seen from behind and to one side, is
> writing large capital letters on the whitened board with a stick of
> charcoal (the letters faint and unreadable). Seven pupils on stools watch
> the board; one boy with curly hair leans back and looks at the ceiling
> instead. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**5 · Part II · The Machinery of Greek (p. 67)**
> [Style block] Pupils on stools bent over the wax tablets on their knees,
> copying, styluses moving. The bearded teacher walks slowly between the
> stools, looking down over one pupil's shoulder with his hands behind his
> back. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**6 · Part III · The World of the Classics (p. 119)**
> [Style block] The bearded teacher sits on a chair reading aloud from an
> open scroll held in both hands. The pupils sit around him on stools and
> on the floor, listening; one girl with long hair tied back leans forward,
> chin on her hands, absorbed. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**7 · Part IV · Greek Becomes a Language of Scripture (p. 179)**
> [Style block] The bearded teacher holds open a thick bound book with
> wooden covers (a late-Roman codex), showing it to two pupils, a
> round-faced boy and a tall thin boy, who look closely at the page. The
> page shows only faint indistinct lines. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**8 · Part V · Becoming a Reader (p. 239)**
> [Style block] In the sunny courtyard, a boy stands reading aloud from an
> unrolled scroll. The other pupils sit on the steps and the low wall,
> listening. The bearded teacher stands to one side against a column, arms
> folded, listening without interrupting. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**9 · Part VI · Writing What You Read (p. 298)**
> [Style block] Close view: the bearded teacher leans over a seated pupil's
> shoulder and points with his finger at a word on her wax tablet; she
> holds her stylus ready, its flat end turned to smooth out a mistake. Only
> the two figures. Aspect ratio 4:3.

## Lesson ends
