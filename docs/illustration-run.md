# Illustration prompts: the run that works

The working method for Gemini, found by trial in October 2026. It
replaces the scene prompts in `illustration-prompts.md` for actual use;
that file still explains the reasoning, the jokes and the sizes.

## Method

1. Use the **Pro** model (mode picker next to the input box), not Flash.
   Flash drifts: faces change, the teacher appears twice, beards turn white.
2. **Attach `assets/illustrations/character-sheet.jpg` to every prompt**,
   not only the first. Gemini looks at an attachment far more closely than
   at an image earlier in the chat.
3. Paste the **cast** paragraph and the **style** paragraph below, then the
   scene. Every scene names the pupils, so each is matched to the sheet.
4. Check each picture before the next. If someone is wrong, reply
   *"Edit this last image only: … Keep everything else exactly the same."*
   That fixed the wrong boy in scene 3 at the first try.
5. Never press Escape while Gemini is drawing: it cancels the picture.
6. The sheet's poses travel with the characters: Felix tends to arrive with
   a tablet on his head and Theodosius with a drop of sweat. Say so in the
   scene ("Felix has nothing on his head") when the joke does not need it,
   or let the caption account for it.
7. Ask for believable physics: a pupil tipping his stool back should have
   his shoulders against the wall, or he reads as falling over.
8. Greek on the board is possible if it is short and named exactly
   ("the two words ὁ λόγος, clearly legible"); check the accents.
9. A Pro account allowed about five or six pictures a day; plan on several
   days, or several accounts.

## Cast (paste first)

> Use the attached character sheet as the exact model for every person who appears. From left to right it shows: Theodoros, the teacher (dark brown beard going a little grey, never white; receding hair; cloak over one shoulder); Marcus (short curly hair, smug); Julia (braided pinned-up hair); Felix (the smallest, wild spiky hair); Sabina (long ponytail); Quintus (very tall and thin); Livia (hair in a bun, sleepy half-lidded eyes); Theodosius (round face, short hair); Aelia (dark curly hair with a headband); and the tabby cat. Copy faces, hair, builds and clothes exactly. Theodoros is the only adult and appears exactly once.

## Style (paste second)

> Create an image. Same black-and-white animated-sitcom style as the sheet: even inked outlines, flat white fills, flat grey shadows, pinprick pupils, deadpan faces. Setting: a plain schoolroom in a house in Rome, AD 400, with low wooden stools, wax tablets, a whitened board on an easel and scrolls on a shelf. No desks or tables. No text, letters or zzz anywhere. New composition; do not reuse any earlier picture.

## Scenes

**1 · To the Student (end, p. 6)**
> Scene: Livia sits on a stool staring at the wax tablet on her knees with sudden delight: she has just read it by herself. Behind her Felix has fallen asleep with his cheek stuck to his own tablet. Theodoros, far in the background, notices Livia and ignores Felix. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**2 · The School on the Caelian Hill (end, p. 8)**
> Scene: The whole schoolroom seen from the courtyard doorway: Theodoros beside the board, all eight pupils on stools in a loose half-circle with tablets on their knees. The gag: Felix dangles a stylus on a thread behind his stool and the cat is crouched to pounce on it. Theodoros is very deliberately not looking. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**3 · How This Course Pronounces Greek (end, p. 13)**
> Scene: Theodoros holds a small lit oil lamp in front of his mouth and breathes a sound at it; the flame leans politely away. Beside him Felix tries the same with puffed-out cheeks and blows his own lamp out completely, with a curl of smoke. Sabina and Aelia lean back in mock alarm. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**4 · Part I · The Strange Language (p. 16)**
> Scene: Theodoros, seen from behind and to one side, draws large faint unreadable marks on the board. Seven pupils on stools watch with glazed, half-lidded eyes. The gag: Marcus has tipped his stool back on two legs and stares at the ceiling with a blank face, balanced at the exact point of falling over, while Julia side-eyes him. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**5 · Part II · The Machinery of Greek (p. 67)**
> Scene: Pupils on stools bend over the tablets on their knees, copying. Theodoros walks slowly between the stools, hands behind his back. The gag: as he passes, Felix hurriedly covers with his elbow a doodle on his tablet of a bearded face with a long nose that looks exactly like Theodoros. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**6 · Part III · The World of the Classics (p. 119)**
> Scene: Theodoros sits reading aloud from an open scroll. Sabina leans forward, completely absorbed. The gag: behind his back Felix covers one eye with his hand and looms over Theodosius, playing a one-eyed giant, while Theodosius pretends not to be scared. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**7 · Part IV · Greek Becomes a Language of Scripture (p. 179)**
> Scene: Theodoros holds open a thick book with wooden covers (a late-Roman codex) to show the class. Quintus stands right in front, seeing it perfectly. The gag: Theodosius cannot see past him, so he has climbed onto a stool behind Quintus and peers over his shoulder on tiptoe, wobbling. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**8 · Part V · Becoming a Reader (p. 239)**
> Scene: In the courtyard, Quintus stands reading aloud from an unrolled scroll. The other pupils sit on the steps; Theodoros leans against a column, arms folded. The gag: on the wall directly above Quintus sits the cat, looking down at him like a strict examiner. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**9 · Part VI · Writing What You Read (p. 298)**
> Scene: Close view: Theodoros leans over Livia's shoulder and points with scholarly interest at her wax tablet, where there is a neat cat's paw print pressed into the wax. Livia stares straight at the viewer, flat-eyed and outraged. The cat strolls away, tail up, smug. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**10 · Lesson 1, The Unwelcome Tablet (p. 23)**
> Scene: Theodoros holds up Marcus's wax tablet and looks at it in a long, terrible silence. Marcus sits with an innocent face. The gag: the whole class has frozen, and Felix watches through his fingers as if at a gladiator show. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**11 · Lesson 2, The Case of the Missing Letters (p. 30)**
> Scene: End of the lesson. Livia asks Theodoros a quiet, pointed question and he looks caught out. The gag: in the background, unnoticed, the cat sits squarely on someone's open wax tablet, smudging it. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**12 · Lesson 3, Names and Nouns (p. 38)**
> Scene: Pupils tie small blank labels onto things in the room: a stool, a tablet, the window. Felix pins a blank label onto Quintus, who stands like a statue, bearing it with dignity. The gag: the cat has a label tied to its tail and is spinning round trying to catch it. Theodoros watches, trying not to smile. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**13 · Lesson 4, Who Did What? (p. 47)**
> Scene: The classroom set up as a law court. Sabina sits as judge on two stools stacked on top of each other, holding a broom like a staff of office. Felix stands as the witness, hand on heart, overacting wildly. Marcus, the accused, yawns. Theodoros leans on the wall, arms folded. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**14 · Lesson 5, The Verb Appears (p. 56)**
> Scene: The pupils take turns standing to introduce themselves. The gag: it is Felix's turn and he is standing on top of his stool to look taller, chest out. Theodoros, seated, silently points him back down to the floor. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**15 · Lesson 6, The First Real Sentence (p. 66)**
> Scene: End of the lesson. Theodoros wipes the board clean with a cloth, leaving one small patch in the corner. The gag: the cat sits on top of the easel with one paw planted on that last corner, guarding it and glaring at the cloth. Pupils leave in the background. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**16 · Lesson 7, The Article's Many Disguises (p. 75)**
> Scene: Marcus argues with Theodoros, flinging his arm out in a grand gesture so that his own wax tablet flies off his knee. Beside him Julia calmly adds another tally mark to her tablet without looking up. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**17 · Lesson 9, The Dative and the Recipient (p. 92)**
> Scene: Felix presents a folded letter to Theodoros with enormous ceremony, bowing so low that his nose nearly touches the floor. Theodoros accepts it with equally exaggerated gravity, one eyebrow raised. Classmates grin behind their tablets. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**18 · Lesson 11, One Verb, Several Clues (p. 109)**
> Scene: Years later: Marcus as a grown man of about 30, same curly hair, now a teacher himself, stands at the window of a plain provincial schoolroom, back half-turned to a small class of children on stools, quietly moved. In the front row one small boy balances a stylus on his nose. Theodoros does not appear. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**19 · Lesson 12, The First Reading Challenge (p. 118)**
> Scene: Two small teams of pupils huddle over their wax tablets, whispering. Theodoros stands between them as judge. The gag: Felix cranes across to peek at the other team's tablet and Livia hauls him back by the collar without even looking up. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**20 · Lesson 13, The Past Comes Alive (p. 127)**
> Scene: After the lesson, Quintus explains a big idea to Theodoros with a grand philosopher's gesture. Theodoros listens, amused. The gag: Quintus's cloak has slipped off onto the floor and the cat is already curled up asleep in it. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**21 · Lesson 14, The Aorist Arrives (p. 136)**
> Scene: Marcus writes carefully on the back of his wax tablet while Theodoros glances down at it. The gag: behind them the cat has just leapt off the shelf of scrolls, and a tablet it knocked off is caught in mid-air, about to hit the floor. Neither of them has noticed. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**22 · Lesson 15, The Words of Homer (p. 146)**
> Scene: By the shelf of scrolls, Sabina begs Theodoros for something, wearing an upturned bowl as a warrior's helmet and holding a stylus like a spear. Theodoros clutches a battered old scroll protectively to his chest. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**23 · Lesson 16, Odysseus and the Problem of Cleverness (p. 157)**
> Scene: Felix has his hand up to ask a question; he has drawn a single large eye on his forehead in charcoal. Theodoros looks at him with weary patience. The class is laughing. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**24 · Lesson 18, The Trial of a Translation (p. 178)**
> Scene: The classroom as a trial, Theodoros seated as judge. At the back Julia has stood up to speak, nervous but steady, and every head has turned to her. The gag: in the middle of the room Marcus is frozen in a grand orator's pose, arm still raised, abandoned by his audience. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**25 · Lesson 19, The Familiar Words Return (p. 188)**
> Scene: Through the open doorway, the cat sits on the courtyard wall like an emperor on a throne. Felix bows deeply to it. Theodoros stands beside him, gesturing at the cat as if introducing royalty. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**26 · Lesson 20, The Greek of the Septuagint (p. 197)**
> Scene: Aelia counts something on her fingers while talking to Theodoros, who strokes his beard. The gag: she has run out of fingers and has grabbed Felix's hand to keep counting on his. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**27 · Lesson 21, The Sermon on the Mount (p. 206)**
> Scene: Felix, very pleased with himself, has counted to eight on his fingers and, to be sure, has taken off his sandals and is checking on his toes. Theodoros stands over him with a patient, challenging look. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**28 · Lesson 23, Attic and Koine Face to Face (p. 228)**
> Scene: End of the lesson. Theodoros sits at ease on the end of a bench, sandals off, eating figs from a bowl, talking to Felix, who stares at this unexpected sight of the teacher off duty. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**29 · Lesson 24, Two Worlds, One Language Family (p. 238)**
> Scene: In the courtyard Quintus and Theodoros talk seriously over a scroll held partly open between them. The gag: the loose end of the scroll dangles down and the cat below is batting at it with one paw. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**30 · Lesson 25, The Grammar of Questions (p. 249)**
> Scene: Livia reads aloud from her tablet. Theodoros, smiling, gestures toward her as if thanking her in front of the class. The gag: Marcus in the front row looks utterly scandalised that someone is being thanked. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**31 · Lesson 27, The Translator's Dilemma (p. 267)**
> Scene: Julia stands before Theodoros, a little defensive. Beside her Livia unrolls a scroll. The gag: the scroll has unrolled much too far, right across the floor and out through the door, and the cat is chasing the end of it. Aspect ratio 3:1.

**32 · Lesson 28, A Greek Letter from Rome (p. 276)**
> Scene: Aelia writes on a sheet of papyrus with a reed pen while Theodoros reads over her shoulder, nodding. The gag: behind them Felix is solemnly handing a folded letter of his own to the cat, which is reading it. Aspect ratio 2:1.

**33 · Lesson 30, The Final Examination (p. 297)**
> Scene: Evening, long shadows. Theodoros goes to put a last scroll on the shelf. Felix waits at the door to go home. The gag: the slot where the scroll belongs is already taken by the cat, curled up asleep in it. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**34 · Lesson 31, The Voice That Looks Back (p. 309)**
> Scene: Felix, soaking wet, hair plastered flat, dripping into a puddle around his stool, writes grimly on his wax tablet while Theodoros reads over his shoulder, expressionless. Through the open doorway, on the courtyard wall, the cat sits perfectly dry and fluffy, washing a paw, smug. Aspect ratio 3:1.

**35 · Lesson 32, What Will Be (p. 322)**
> Scene: An empty classroom after the lesson. Theodoros takes a wax tablet down from the shelf and studies it. Marcus waits with an innocent face and his fingers crossed behind his back. Aspect ratio 4:3.

**36 · Lesson 35, What Might Be (p. 354)**
> Scene: At the courtyard door Quintus turns back to ask a question. Theodoros answers him, carrying a wobbling stack of wax tablets taller than his head, with the cat sitting calmly on top. Aspect ratio 3:2.

**37 · Lesson 36, The Old Verbs (p. 365)**
> Scene: Ten years later: Julia as a young woman of about 24, same braided pinned-up hair, now a teacher herself, stands in her own small schoolroom reading a very short letter, quietly moved. Behind her a boy with short curly hair has tipped his stool back on two legs and stares at the ceiling, exactly as her brother once did. Theodoros does not appear. Aspect ratio 3:1.
