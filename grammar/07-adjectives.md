# Adjectives

An adjective agrees with its noun in **gender, number and case**. It does
not have to agree in declension: **ὁ ἀγαθὸς πολίτης** is a second-
declension adjective on a first-declension noun, and is correct.

Most adjectives use the first and second declensions. A substantial group
uses the third.

## Adjectives of the first and second declension

The masculine and neuter decline like **λόγος** and **δῶρον**, the
feminine like **ψυχή**.

| | masc. | fem. | neut. | | masc. | fem. | neut. |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:-:|:--|:--|:--|
| | **Singular** | | | | **Plural** | | |
| nom. | **ἀγαθός** | **ἀγαθή** | **ἀγαθόν** | | **ἀγαθοί** | **ἀγαθαί** | **ἀγαθά** |
| gen. | **ἀγαθοῦ** | **ἀγαθῆς** | **ἀγαθοῦ** | | **ἀγαθῶν** | **ἀγαθῶν** | **ἀγαθῶν** |
| dat. | **ἀγαθῷ** | **ἀγαθῇ** | **ἀγαθῷ** | | **ἀγαθοῖς** | **ἀγαθαῖς** | **ἀγαθοῖς** |
| acc. | **ἀγαθόν** | **ἀγαθήν** | **ἀγαθόν** | | **ἀγαθούς** | **ἀγαθάς** | **ἀγαθά** |
| voc. | **ἀγαθέ** | **ἀγαθή** | **ἀγαθόν** | | **ἀγαθοί** | **ἀγαθαί** | **ἀγαθά** |

**When the stem ends in ε, ι or ρ the feminine has -ᾱ, not -η**, by the
same rule that governs the first-declension noun (§4.2):
**δίκαιος, δικαίᾱ, δίκαιον** *just* · **μικρός, μικρᾱ́, μικρόν** *small* ·
**ἄξιος, ἀξίᾱ, ἄξιον** *worthy*.

::: note
**The accent of an adjective is the noun's accent, not the verb's.** It is
persistent (§2.5): it stays where the masculine nominative singular puts
it, and moves only when Rules 1–3 force it.

One consequence catches every beginner. §4.5 says that a first-declension
**noun** always has **-ῶν** in the genitive plural. An adjective's feminine
does **not** obey that rule: it keeps the adjective's own accent. So
**δίκαιος** gives feminine genitive plural **δικαίων**, not *δικαιῶν*,
even though **ἡ οἰκίᾱ** gives **οἰκιῶν**. A feminine adjective is accented
as its adjective is accented, never as a noun of the same shape would be.

**ἀγαθῶν** hides this, because there the two rules agree.
:::

## Two-termination adjectives

A large group has **no separate feminine**: the masculine form does duty
for both genders, and only two sets of endings exist.

| | masc. / fem. | neut. | | masc. / fem. | neut. |
|:--|:--|:--|:-:|:--|:--|
| | **Singular** | | | **Plural** | |
| nom. | **ἄδικος** | **ἄδικον** | | **ἄδικοι** | **ἄδικα** |
| gen. | **ἀδίκου** | **ἀδίκου** | | **ἀδίκων** | **ἀδίκων** |
| dat. | **ἀδίκῳ** | **ἀδίκῳ** | | **ἀδίκοις** | **ἀδίκοις** |
| acc. | **ἄδικον** | **ἄδικον** | | **ἀδίκους** | **ἄδικα** |

Chiefly **compounds** — adjectives built from a preposition or **ἀ-**
privative plus a noun or verb: **ἄδικος** *unjust* (**ἀ-** + **δίκη**) ·
**ἀθάνατος** *deathless* · **ἄπειρος** *inexperienced* ·
**ἔνδοξος** *famous* · **βάρβαρος** *foreign*.

So **ἡ ἄδικος γυνή** is right and *ἡ ἀδίκη γυνή* is wrong. The rule is
not absolute — poetry and some prose adjectives waver — but for a reader
it is reliable: a feminine noun with an apparently masculine adjective is
usually a compound, not an error.

## Contract adjectives

A small class contracts, exactly as the contract nouns of §5.6 do, and the
circumflex is again the mark of it.

| | masc. | fem. | neut. |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| nom. sg. | **χρυσοῦς** | **χρυσῆ** | **χρυσοῦν** |
| gen. sg. | **χρυσοῦ** | **χρυσῆς** | **χρυσοῦ** |
| dat. sg. | **χρυσῷ** | **χρυσῇ** | **χρυσῷ** |
| acc. sg. | **χρυσοῦν** | **χρυσῆν** | **χρυσοῦν** |
| nom. pl. | **χρυσοῖ** | **χρυσαῖ** | **χρυσᾶ** |

From **χρύσεος** *golden*. Like it: **ἀργυροῦς** *of silver* ·
**ἁπλοῦς** *simple* · **διπλοῦς** *double*. Adjectives of material are the
largest group.

## Third-declension adjectives

Four types, and all four are common.

**In -ων, -ον** (stem in **-ον-**), two terminations:

| | masc. / fem. | neut. | | masc. / fem. | neut. |
|:--|:--|:--|:-:|:--|:--|
| nom. | **εὐδαίμων** | **εὔδαιμον** | | **εὐδαίμονες** | **εὐδαίμονα** |
| gen. | **εὐδαίμονος** | **εὐδαίμονος** | | **εὐδαιμόνων** | **εὐδαιμόνων** |
| dat. | **εὐδαίμονι** | **εὐδαίμονι** | | **εὐδαίμοσι(ν)** | **εὐδαίμοσι(ν)** |
| acc. | **εὐδαίμονα** | **εὔδαιμον** | | **εὐδαίμονας** | **εὐδαίμονα** |

**In -ης, -ες** (sigma stem, as **γένος** at §6.6), two terminations:

| | masc. / fem. | neut. | | masc. / fem. | neut. |
|:--|:--|:--|:-:|:--|:--|
| nom. | **ἀληθής** | **ἀληθές** | | **ἀληθεῖς** | **ἀληθῆ** |
| gen. | **ἀληθοῦς** | **ἀληθοῦς** | | **ἀληθῶν** | **ἀληθῶν** |
| dat. | **ἀληθεῖ** | **ἀληθεῖ** | | **ἀληθέσι(ν)** | **ἀληθέσι(ν)** |
| acc. | **ἀληθῆ** | **ἀληθές** | | **ἀληθεῖς** | **ἀληθῆ** |

**πᾶς, πᾶσα, πᾶν** *all, every* — third declension in the masculine and
neuter, first in the feminine:

| | masc. | fem. | neut. | | masc. | fem. | neut. |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:-:|:--|:--|:--|
| nom. | **πᾶς** | **πᾶσα** | **πᾶν** | | **πάντες** | **πᾶσαι** | **πάντα** |
| gen. | **παντός** | **πάσης** | **παντός** | | **πάντων** | **πασῶν** | **πάντων** |
| dat. | **παντί** | **πάσῃ** | **παντί** | | **πᾶσι(ν)** | **πάσαις** | **πᾶσι(ν)** |
| acc. | **πάντα** | **πᾶσαν** | **πᾶν** | | **πάντας** | **πάσας** | **πάντα** |

**μέγας** *great* and **πολύς** *much, many* are irregular in the
nominative and accusative singular of the masculine and neuter, and
regular everywhere else, where they decline as though from
**μεγαλο-** and **πολλο-**.

| | **μέγας, μεγάλη, μέγα** | **πολύς, πολλή, πολύ** |
|:--|:--|:--|
| nom. sg. m. | **μέγας** | **πολύς** |
| acc. sg. m. | **μέγαν** | **πολύν** |
| nom. / acc. sg. n. | **μέγα** | **πολύ** |
| gen. sg. m. | **μεγάλου** | **πολλοῦ** |
| nom. pl. m. | **μεγάλοι** | **πολλοί** |

## Attributive and predicate position

Fully treated at §3.3, because the work is done by the article and not by
the adjective. In summary:

> **ὁ ἀγαθὸς ἄνθρωπος** / **ὁ ἄνθρωπος ὁ ἀγαθός** — *the good man*
> **ὁ ἄνθρωπος ἀγαθός** / **ἀγαθὸς ὁ ἄνθρωπος** — *the man is good*

The adjective inside the article's grip modifies; the adjective outside it
predicates. No verb is needed for the second.

## The adjective used as a noun

With an article and no noun, an adjective becomes one. The gender and
number of the article say who or what is meant (§3.4).

| | |
|:--|:--|
| **οἱ ἀγαθοί** | the good men, good people |
| **αἱ ἀγαθαί** | the good women |
| **τὸ καλόν** | the beautiful, beauty as such |
| **τὰ καλά** | beautiful things |
| **οἱ πολλοί** | the many, the majority |
| **τὰ τῶν Ἑλλήνων** | the affairs of the Greeks |

The neuter singular is how Greek names an abstraction, and it is the
reason so much Greek philosophy is written in neuter articles.

## Comparison

**More** is **-τερος, -τέρᾱ, -τερον**; **most** is **-τατος, -τάτη,
-τατον**. Both decline as ordinary first-and-second-declension adjectives.

| | positive | comparative | superlative |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| | **δίκαιος** *just* | **δικαιότερος** | **δικαιότατος** |
| | **νέος** *young* | **νεώτερος** | **νεώτατος** |
| | **ἀληθής** *true* | **ἀληθέστερος** | **ἀληθέστατος** |

**The ο lengthens to ω when the syllable before it is short**:
**σοφός → σοφώτερος**, but **δίκαιος → δικαιότερος**, where **-και-** is
long and no lengthening occurs. The rule is the same one that governs
accentuation — Greek counting syllable weight — and not a separate fact.

A second and older pattern, **-ίων, -ιστος**, survives in the irregulars
of §7.8 and in a handful of others: **ἡδύς** *sweet* →
**ἡδίων, ἥδιστος**; **ταχύς** *swift* → **θάττων, τάχιστος**.

::: note
**The comparative does not always compare.** With no second term in sight,
a Greek comparative often means *rather* or *too*:
**νεώτερος** is *rather young* as readily as *younger*, and
**ψυχρότερον τὸ ὕδωρ** may be *the water is too cold*.

The superlative behaves likewise: **σοφώτατος** with no field of
comparison is *very wise*. Grammars call this the *elative* use, and it is
common enough that translating every superlative as *most* will
mislead you.
:::

## Irregular comparison

Five sets, all high-frequency, all worth memorising whole. Several have two
comparatives with different senses.

| | positive | comparative | superlative |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| good | **ἀγαθός** | **ἀμείνων**, **βελτίων**, **κρείττων** | **ἄριστος**, **βέλτιστος**, **κράτιστος** |
| bad | **κακός** | **κακίων**, **χείρων** | **κάκιστος**, **χείριστος** |
| great | **μέγας** | **μείζων** | **μέγιστος** |
| much | **πολύς** | **πλείων**, **πλέων** | **πλεῖστος** |
| small | **μικρός** | **ἐλάττων**, **μείων** | **ἐλάχιστος** |

**ἀμείνων** is *better* in ability or bravery, **βελτίων** in moral
quality, **κρείττων** in strength or power. The distinction is real in
Plato and worth noticing; translating all three as *better* loses an
argument's point more often than one would expect.

The **-ων** comparatives decline as third-declension adjectives of the
**εὐδαίμων** type, with alternative contracted forms in the accusative
singular and the nominative and accusative plural:
**μείζω** beside **μείζονα**, **μείζους** beside **μείζονες**.

## The genitive of comparison, and ἤ

Greek has two ways to say *than*, and they are freely interchangeable.

**With ἤ**, and the two things compared in the same case:

> **ὁ πατὴρ σοφώτερός ἐστιν ἢ ὁ υἱός** — *the father is wiser than the
> son* (both nominative)

**With the genitive**, and no word for *than* at all:

> **ὁ πατὴρ σοφώτερός ἐστι τοῦ υἱοῦ** — same sense, **τοῦ υἱοῦ**
> genitive

The genitive construction is the older and the tighter, and it is the one
that surprises a reader coming from Latin, where the ablative does the same
work. Its limit: it can only be used when the second term would otherwise
have stood in the nominative or accusative. Everywhere else **ἤ** is
required.

**A superlative takes a genitive of the group compared**:

> **σοφώτατος τῶν ἀνθρώπων** — *wisest of men*
> **ἄριστος πάντων** — *best of all*

And a superlative is strengthened by **ὡς** or **ὅτι**: **ὡς τάχιστα**,
*as quickly as possible*.
