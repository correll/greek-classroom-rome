# The Verb: An Overview

Before any paradigm, the categories. A Greek verb form carries five pieces of information at once, and a reader who knows which five can decode an unfamiliar form.

## Planned subsections

1. Person and number: three persons, three numbers (the dual is rare)
2. Voice: active, middle, passive — and why the middle has no English equivalent
3. Mood: indicative, subjunctive, optative, imperative; the infinitive and participle as non-finite forms
4. **Aspect, not tense**: the central idea. Imperfective, aoristic, and perfective, and how aspect and time interact in and outside the indicative
5. The principal parts, and why six of them are needed
6. Thematic (**-ω**) and athematic (**-μι**) conjugations
7. The augment and reduplication: what each one marks
8. Recessive accent in the verb (§2.7)
