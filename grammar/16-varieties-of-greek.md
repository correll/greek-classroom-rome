# Attic, Homeric, and Koine

The course reads Greek from three periods and two dialects. This section states the differences plainly rather than letting a student discover them by being confused.

## Planned subsections

1. **The written text.** Majuscule script, *scriptio continua*, no accents or breathings before the Alexandrians; what a modern edition has added, and when
2. **Homeric Greek.** An artificial literary dialect, chiefly Ionic with Aeolic elements, archaic by the classical period: uncontracted forms, **-οιο** and **-άων** genitives, **-εσσι** datives, the article as demonstrative, **τε** as a generalising particle, tmesis, and the metrical traces of the lost digamma
3. **Koine.** The common Greek of the Hellenistic and Roman world: the dual and optative receding, **ἵνα** clauses displacing the infinitive, simpler sentence structure, the dative under pressure from prepositional phrases, vocabulary shifts, and Semitic influence in translation Greek
4. **Metre.** Dactylic hexameter, enough to hear a line of Homer as verse; quantity, not stress; the caesura
5. A comparative table: the same sentence as it would appear in Homeric, Attic, and Koine Greek
