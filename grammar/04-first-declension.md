# The First Declension

The first declension contains nouns whose stem ends in **-ᾱ**. Most are
feminine; a small and important group is masculine.

## Feminine in -η

Nouns whose stem vowel appears as **η** throughout the singular.

| | **ἡ ψυχή** *soul* | |
|:--|:--|:--|
| | **Singular** | **Plural** |
| nominative | **ψυχή** | **ψυχαί** |
| genitive | **ψυχῆς** | **ψυχῶν** |
| dative | **ψυχῇ** | **ψυχαῖς** |
| accusative | **ψυχήν** | **ψυχάς** |
| vocative | **ψυχή** | **ψυχαί** |

Like **ψυχή**: **ἡ ἀρχή** *beginning, rule* · **ἡ γραφή** *writing* ·
**ἡ τιμή** *honour* · **ἡ φωνή** *voice* · **ἡ τέχνη** *skill*.

## Feminine in -ᾱ

After **ε**, **ι**, or **ρ**, the stem vowel appears as long **ᾱ** rather
than **η**, throughout the singular. This is a purely phonetic rule of
Attic and it is regular.

| | **ἡ οἰκίᾱ** *house* | |
|:--|:--|:--|
| | **Singular** | **Plural** |
| nominative | **οἰκίᾱ** | **οἰκίαι** |
| genitive | **οἰκίᾱς** | **οἰκιῶν** |
| dative | **οἰκίᾳ** | **οἰκίαις** |
| accusative | **οἰκίᾱν** | **οἰκίᾱς** |
| vocative | **οἰκίᾱ** | **οἰκίαι** |

Like **οἰκία**: **ἡ ἡμέρᾱ** *day* · **ἡ χώρᾱ** *land* · **ἡ σοφίᾱ**
*wisdom* · **ἡ ἀλήθειᾰ** (but see below).

::: note
**The ε, ι, ρ rule.** If the letter before the ending is **ε**, **ι**, or
**ρ**, the first declension keeps **ᾱ**; otherwise it shows **η**. Compare
**ἡ χώρᾱ** (after **ρ**) with **ἡ τιμή** (after **μ**).

In Ionic the **ᾱ** became **η** everywhere, which is why Herodotus writes
**χώρη** where an Athenian writes **χώρᾱ**, and why Homer has **θεή** for
Attic **θεά** (§16.2).
:::

## Feminine in short -ᾰ

A third group has a **short** alpha in the nominative and accusative
singular, and **η** in the genitive and dative. These are nouns whose stem
ends in a double consonant or a cluster.

| | **ἡ θάλαττᾰ** *sea* | |
|:--|:--|:--|
| | **Singular** | **Plural** |
| nominative | **θάλαττᾰ** | **θάλατται** |
| genitive | **θαλάττης** | **θαλαττῶν** |
| dative | **θαλάττῃ** | **θαλάτταις** |
| accusative | **θάλαττᾰν** | **θαλάττᾱς** |
| vocative | **θάλαττᾰ** | **θάλατται** |

Like **θάλαττα**: **ἡ γλῶττᾰ** *tongue* · **ἡ μοῦσᾰ** *Muse* ·
**ἡ δόξᾰ** *opinion, reputation* · **ἡ ἀλήθειᾰ** *truth*.

Note that the short alpha affects the accent: **θάλαττα** can keep its
accent on the antepenult because the ultima is short (§2.3, Rule 2), but
**θαλάττης** cannot.

## Masculine

A group of masculine nouns, chiefly agent nouns and ethnic names, declines
in the first declension with two differences: the nominative singular adds
**-ς**, and the genitive singular is **-ου**, borrowed from the second
declension.

| | **ὁ πολίτης** *citizen* | **ὁ νεανίᾱς** *young man* |
|:--|:--|:--|
| nominative | **πολίτης** | **νεανίᾱς** |
| genitive | **πολίτου** | **νεανίου** |
| dative | **πολίτῃ** | **νεανίᾳ** |
| accusative | **πολίτην** | **νεανίᾱν** |
| vocative | **πολῖτα** | **νεανίᾱ** |
| nom. plural | **πολῖται** | **νεανίαι** |
| gen. plural | **πολιτῶν** | **νεανιῶν** |
| dat. plural | **πολίταις** | **νεανίαις** |
| acc. plural | **πολίτᾱς** | **νεανίᾱς** |

Like **πολίτης**: **ὁ ναύτης** *sailor* · **ὁ στρατιώτης** *soldier* ·
**ὁ μαθητής** *student* · **ὁ δεσπότης** *master* · **ὁ Πέρσης** *Persian*.

::: note
**The plural is the same for everyone.** All four types above share an
identical plural in **-αι, -ῶν, -αις, -ᾱς**. Only the singular varies.
Learn the plural once.
:::

## The genitive plural

The genitive plural of the first declension is **always -ῶν**, with a
circumflex, in every noun of the declension, regardless of where the
accent stands elsewhere.

> **ψυχή → ψυχῶν** · **θάλαττα → θαλαττῶν** · **πολίτης → πολιτῶν** ·
> **μοῦσα → μουσῶν**

The circumflex is the residue of a contraction (**-άων → -ῶν**) and it is
one of the most reliable diagnostics in the language: a word ending in
**-ῶν** with a circumflex is very probably a first-declension genitive
plural.

An exception is the feminine of adjectives declined like **-ος -η -ον**,
which follow the accent of their masculine (§7.1), so **ἀγαθῶν** not
*ἀγαθῶν* — the accent happens to coincide, but for a different reason.

## The vocative

Normally identical to the nominative. Two exceptions among the masculines:

- Nouns in **-της** have a short **-ᾰ** with recessive accent:
  **ὦ πολῖτα**, **ὦ ναῦτα**, **ὦ δέσποτα**.
- Compounds and ethnic names generally follow the same pattern.

## Accent in the first declension

Three rules, applied in this order.

1. The accent is **persistent** — it stays where the nominative puts it,
   as far as §2.3 allows.
2. Genitive and dative, singular and plural, take a **circumflex on the
   ultima** if the accent is on the ultima at all: **ψυχῆς**, **ψυχῇ**,
   **ψυχαῖς**, but accusative **ψυχάς** with an acute.
3. The genitive plural is **-ῶν** always (§4.5).

> **ἡ ψυχή** — accent on the ultima throughout:
> **ψυχή, ψυχῆς, ψυχῇ, ψυχήν · ψυχαί, ψυχῶν, ψυχαῖς, ψυχάς**
>
> **ἡ θάλαττα** — accent on the antepenult where the ultima permits:
> **θάλαττα, θαλάττης, θαλάττῃ, θάλατταν · θάλατται, θαλαττῶν,
> θαλάτταις, θαλάττας**
