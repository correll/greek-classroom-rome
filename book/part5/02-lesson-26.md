# Commands, Advice, and Persuasion

::: {.story time="5 min"}
Marcus mounts a sustained argument that homework is philosophically unnecessary. It is his best work of the year. The class is instructed to evaluate the Greek and the reasoning separately, and discovers that the grammar is flawless and the argument is not.

> **To be written.** The scene above is the plan; the prose is not yet

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** The imperative, present and aorist; prohibition with **μή**; expressions of obligation (**δεῖ**, **χρή**); the difference between telling, advising, and persuading.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm
> table, two or three worked examples, and a **From Latin** box wherever
> the Latin parallel either helps or misleads.
:::

::: vocab
**ἄκουε** listen! · **λέγε** speak! · **μή** not (with commands) · **δεῖ** it is necessary · **χρή** one ought · **πείθω** I persuade

> **To be checked.** Accentuation, vowel length, and the article for every
> noun must be verified against a lexicon before this lesson is taught.
:::
