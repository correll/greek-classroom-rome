# What Makes a Just Person?

::: {.story time="5 min"}
Quintus finally gets his Plato. He has been looking forward to this since Lesson 5 and discovers that reading philosophy in Greek is mostly a matter of keeping track of very small words.

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** Connectors and sentence relationships: **μέν … δέ**, **γάρ**, **οὖν**, **ἀλλά**; the demonstrative and relative pronouns; how Greek signposts an argument.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm
> table, two or three worked examples, and a **From Latin** box wherever
> the Latin parallel either helps or misleads.
:::

::: vocab
**δίκαιος** just · **ἄδικος** unjust · **ἡ δικαιοσύνη** justice · **μέν … δέ** on the one hand … on the other · **γάρ** for · **οὖν** therefore

> **To be checked.** Accentuation, vowel length, and the article for every

::: {.voice time="10 min"}
**Text for this lesson.** A short Platonic exchange with accessible syntax and a real ethical question. The pupils track the argument by its connectors before they translate a word of it.

> **To be selected and verified.** The passage must be quoted exactly from

::: {.question time="5 min"}
