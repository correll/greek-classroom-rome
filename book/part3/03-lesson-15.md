# The Words of Homer

::: {.story time="5 min"}
Theodoros brings out the battered Homer. The pupils have been waiting twelve lessons for this and discover that it is not written in the Greek they have learned.

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** The epic dialect: uncontracted forms, the digamma's ghost, the article used as a pronoun, formulaic epithets; dactylic hexameter heard rather than analysed.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm
> table, two or three worked examples, and a **From Latin** box wherever
> the Latin parallel either helps or misleads.
:::

::: vocab

> **To be checked.** Accentuation, vowel length, and the article for every

::: {.voice time="10 min"}
**Text for this lesson.** The opening lines of the *Odyssey*, with heavy scaffolding. The pupils are told plainly what is Homeric and what is Attic, and why the difference exists.

> **To be selected and verified.** The passage must be quoted exactly from
> a public-domain edition and cited. Vocabulary beyond the course list must
> be glossed in full. Do not present a composed sentence as a quotation.
:::

::: {.question time="5 min"}
Homer was already four hundred years old when Plato read him, and written in a dialect nobody spoke. Why did the Greeks keep him as their central text anyway?
