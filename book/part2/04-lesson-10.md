# Adjectives Must Agree

::: {.story time="5 min"}
Sabina assigns everyone an adjective. The adjectives become steadily less flattering and steadily more grammatically correct, which puts Theodoros in an awkward position.

> **To be written.** The scene above is the plan; the prose is not yet
> drafted. See the course notes in `README.md` for the drafting order.
:::

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** First-and-second-declension adjectives; agreement in gender, number, and case; attributive vs. predicate position and why the difference matters.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm
> table, two or three worked examples, and a **From Latin** box wherever
> the Latin parallel either helps or misleads.
:::

::: {.voice time="10 min"}
**Text for this lesson.** A short line in which the position of the adjective relative to the article changes the meaning — the distinction between *the wise man* and *the man is wise*.

> **To be selected and verified.** The passage must be quoted exactly from
> a public-domain edition and cited. Vocabulary beyond the course list must
> be glossed in full. Do not present a composed sentence as a quotation.
:::

::: {.question time="5 min"}
Greek can say *the man is wise* without any verb at all, just by where the adjective stands. What does that tell you about what a sentence needs?
:::
