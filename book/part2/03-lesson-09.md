# The Dative and the Recipient

::: {.story time="5 min"}
Felix writes a message intended *for* Theodoros, but puts the teacher in the wrong case and so writes a sentence in which Theodoros is doing something rather than receiving it. The result is not insulting, merely bizarre, which Felix finds disappointing.

> **To be written.** The scene above is the plan; the prose is not yet
> drafted. See the course notes in `README.md` for the drafting order.
:::

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** The dative singular and plural; indirect object; dative of means and manner; **ἐν** and **σύν** with the dative.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm

::: {.voice time="10 min"}
**Text for this lesson.** A short passage or inscription in which a gift is given to a god — the dative's most characteristic use in the surviving record.

> **To be selected and verified.** The passage must be quoted exactly from
> a public-domain edition and cited. Vocabulary beyond the course list must
> be glossed in full. Do not present a composed sentence as a quotation.

::: {.question time="5 min"}
The dative covers *to*, *for*, *with*, *by*, and *in*. Is that one case doing many jobs, or several cases that happened to collapse into one shape?
:::
