# The Sermon on the Mount

::: {.story time="5 min"}
A passage every pupil already knows in Latin. Theodoros asks them to read it in Greek and then say what, if anything, is different. Theodosius is first to find something, and it is not what he expected.

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** Imperatives and simple clauses; the nominative in blessing formulas; the characteristic Koine preference for short coordinated sentences.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm

::: {.voice time="10 min"}
**Text for this lesson.** Matthew 5:3-10, the Beatitudes. Short clauses, repeated structure, high memorability; and a translation question in every single line.

> **To be selected and verified.** The passage must be quoted exactly from
> a public-domain edition and cited. Vocabulary beyond the course list must
> be glossed in full. Do not present a composed sentence as a quotation.
:::

::: {.question time="5 min"}
**μακάριος** is often translated *blessed*, which sounds religious, and sometimes *fortunate*, which sounds like luck. The Greek word is older than both. Which English word loses less?
:::
