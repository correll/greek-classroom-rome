# The Familiar Words Return

::: {.story time="5 min"}
Theodosius recognises a Greek word from a passage he has known in Latin since he was six, and discovers that the Greek does not mean quite what the Latin led him to expect.

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** Semantic range and semantic change; how a word's meaning shifts between authors and centuries; the first Koine forms, labelled as such.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm
> table, two or three worked examples, and a **From Latin** box wherever
> the Latin parallel either helps or misleads.
:::

::: {.voice time="10 min"}
**Text for this lesson.** A single word traced across three texts — Attic prose, the Septuagint, and the New Testament — with the meaning shifting each time. **λόγος** is the obvious candidate and the right one.

> **To be selected and verified.** The passage must be quoted exactly from
> a public-domain edition and cited. Vocabulary beyond the course list must
> be glossed in full. Do not present a composed sentence as a quotation.
:::

::: {.question time="5 min"}
Theodosius knew what the word meant in Latin and was wrong about the Greek. Is it harder to learn a word you have never met, or one you think you already know?
:::
