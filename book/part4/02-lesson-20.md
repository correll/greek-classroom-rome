# The Greek of the Septuagint

::: {.story time="5 min"}
Theodoros explains why parts of the Greek Old Testament sound strange to a reader of Plato — and why that strangeness is evidence of care rather than incompetence.

> **To be written.** The scene above is the plan; the prose is not yet
> drafted. See the course notes in `README.md` for the drafting order.
:::

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** Translation Greek: Hebrew word order and idiom showing through; parataxis with **καί**; constructions that are grammatical Greek but unidiomatic Greek.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm
> table, two or three worked examples, and a **From Latin** box wherever
> the Latin parallel either helps or misleads.
:::

::: vocab
**ὁ ποιμήν** shepherd · **ποιμαίνω** I shepherd · **ὁ οἶκος** house (in its Hebraic sense of household, lineage) · **εὐλογέω** I bless · **ὁ αἰών** age, eternity · **ἔλεος** mercy

> **To be checked.** Accentuation, vowel length, and the article for every
> noun must be verified against a lexicon before this lesson is taught.
:::
