# The Good Samaritan

::: {.story time="5 min"}
A compact narrative that the pupils must map before they can translate: who acts, who receives the action, and who merely passes by. Sabina, for once, is the fastest in the room.

> **To be written.** The scene above is the plan; the prose is not yet
> drafted. See the course notes in `README.md` for the drafting order.
:::

::: {.newgreek time="10 min"}
**Grammar for this lesson.** Participles in narrative; chains of aorist participles carrying a story forward; tracking agent and patient through a long sentence.

> **To be written.** Present one main grammatical idea with a paradigm
> table, two or three worked examples, and a **From Latin** box wherever
> the Latin parallel either helps or misleads.
:::

::: {.voice time="10 min"}
**Text for this lesson.** Luke 10:30-37. A story with a clear structure, a sharp ending, and an unusually high density of participles — which is precisely why it is placed here.

> **To be selected and verified.** The passage must be quoted exactly from
> a public-domain edition and cited. Vocabulary beyond the course list must
> be glossed in full. Do not present a composed sentence as a quotation.
:::

::: {.question time="5 min"}
The parable answers a question the questioner did not ask, and refuses the one he did. Does that count as an answer?
